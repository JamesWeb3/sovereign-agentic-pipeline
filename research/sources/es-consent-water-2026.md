## Summary

Environment Southland's public notice recording that the nine resource consents Datagrid NZ
Partnership Limited sought for the Makarewa datacentre were granted by an independent
commissioner on 11 March 2026, without public notification. The notice is the landing page
for the decision and the signed permits themselves, which carry the consented water volumes.
This is the regulator's own record, so it — not media coverage of it — is the primary source
for the water-take, non-notification and consent-granted claims.

## Relevant quotes — the notice

Publication line, top of notice:

> "Date: 13 Mar 2026, 1:31 pm
> Categories: Public notice"

On the decision:

> "The resource consents sought by Datagrid NZ Partnership Limited from Environment
> Southland are granted. All resource consents were determined by an independent
> commissioner."

On non-notification and who was consulted:

> "The application for the consents was not publicly notified because the activities were
> assessed as having no more than minor environmental effects, as per the Resource
> Management Act. Directly affected parties were consulted and they provided written
> approvals – tāngata tiaki of the Ōreti Mātaitai, Invercargill City Council and
> Department of Conservation, and landowners with potentially affected bores."

On the limits of the regulator's remit:

> "Environment Southland is not a construction or energy regulator and does not have any
> regional rules relating to the use of generated electricity."

On the separate land use consent, from a different authority:

> "Southland District Council issued a land use consent to establish a hyperscale
> datacentre including a GXP substation (not notified)."

## Relevant quotes — the commissioner's decision

[Resource Consent Decision APP-20252550.pdf](https://www.es.govt.nz/repository/libraries/id:26gi9ayo517q9stt81sd/hierarchy/environment/consents/documents/Datagrid%20resource%20consents/Resource%20Consent%20Decision%20APP-20252550.pdf)
— "Decision of the Commissioner on behalf of Southland Regional Council / Non-notified
resource consent application".

On what the operational take is for:

> "The applicant will take 7 litres per second of groundwater from multiple bores for cooling
> water and potable supply."
> — section "Groundwater take"

On the cooling method:

> "The system is designed to reduce heat through evaporation of water."
> — section on discharge to air

On the dewatering take:

> "Excavation will extend up to 5 metres below ground level. To enable excavation, the
> applicant intends to pump groundwater to cause a localised lowering of the water table. The
> rate of take is up to 60 litres per second, up to 5,184 m3/day, for a period of up to two
> months."
> — section "Dewatering - water take and discharge"

On allocation headroom:

> "In this case allocation from the groundwater zone, including the applicant's proposed take,
> is less than 9 % of the allocation limit. It is unlikely that dewatering (which will occur
> during the early construction phase) and the water take for cooling water (that will occur
> after commissioning) will occur in the same water year (1 July to 30 June), but even if they
> did, the total allocation would still be less than 10% of the limit."
> — section "Groundwater take"

On effects on neighbouring bores — the drawdown policy exceedance:

> "In some cases, the drawdown effect (the interference effect) is estimated to exceed the 20%
> allowed for by policy (Policy 31 of the RWP, and Policy 22 and Appendix L.3 of the pSWLP)."

How the commissioner resolved that exceedance:

> "In this case the applicant has obtained written approval from the owners of bores E46/0002,
> E46/0960 and E46/0342 with the potential more than 20% drawdown effect. ... Obtaining the
> owner's written approval does not necessarily overcome a policy exceedance. However, the
> purpose of the policy limitation in this case is clearly to protect neighbours' existing
> groundwater supplies. Therefore, I consider than any interference effect on a bore where the
> owner of that bore has given written approval can be disregarded."

And for one further bore:

> "The owners of another bore, E46/0304, which would be subject to about 27% drawdown, have
> advised the applicant that they do not use their bore. I regard that similarly to a
> decommissioned bore that the interference effects policies would not apply to."

Conclusion on the take:

> "Overall, the adverse effects of the groundwater take can be regarded as no more than minor."

On the wastewater scale:

> "The estimated daily rate of discharge is up to 5000 litres per day. That's equivalent in
> nature and scale to the septic tank effluent discharged from 2-3 large households."

The consents granted, verbatim from the decision:

> "The following resource consents are granted subject to conditions:
> AUTH-20252550-01 - the discharge of treated wastewater to land at 370 Flora Road East
> AUTH-20252550-02 - the discharge of contaminants to air at 342, 350, 360 & 370 Flora Road East
> AUTH-20252550-03 - to take and use groundwater for cooling water at 342, 350, 360 & 370 Flora ...
> AUTH-20252550-04 - to take groundwater to dewater a construction area at 342, 350, 360 & 370 ...
> AUTH-20252550-05 - to remove a natural inland wetland at 370 Flora Road East, Makarewa
> AUTH-20252550-06 - to occupy part of the coastal marine area with a datacable at Foveaux Strait
> AUTH-20252550-07 - to install a datacable in the coastal marine area at Foveaux Strait and Ōreti ...
> AUTH-20252550-08 - to discharge water from dewatering at 370 Flora Road East, Makarewa
> AUTH-20252550-09 - to undertake earthworks within 100 metres of a wetland at 370 Flora Road ..."

That is **nine** consents, numbered 01-09, all from Environment Southland. The Southland
District Council land use consent is a tenth authorisation, but from a different authority.
