## Summary

The first climate-first primary source in this corpus: a national political party stating its
own position, naming the Makarewa consent, and asking for a one-year moratorium on consenting
new AI data centres. It supplies a national electricity figure for Datagrid that appears
nowhere else in the corpus, and it makes the non-notified process itself the object of the
complaint.

## Relevant quotes

Chlöe Swarbrick, Green Party Co-leader:

> "Right now, AI data centres can be consented behind closed doors, with no community input."

On Datagrid specifically:

> "The already consented Datagrid hyperscale AI data centre in Southland is expected to use
> about six per cent of our country's total electricity supply, equivalent to the power used
> by more than 300,000 households."

On the consenting process:

> "Currently, Councils get to decide through their planning rules whether to consent these
> developments. These can be on a non-notified basis with no public input required."

On effects claimed from overseas experience:

> "Experience overseas has seen these projects turn local water supply to sludge and suck huge
> amounts of energy, driving up prices for regular people."

On the regulatory alternatives proposed:

> "New York paused new hyperscale data centres for a year to write rules protecting the grid,
> the environment and communities. Australia is moving to require operators to underwrite new
> renewable generation, pay for their own grid connections and water infrastructure, and
> minimise water use."
