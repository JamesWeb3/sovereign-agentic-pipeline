## Read this before citing it

**It is a template, not a single submission.** The coalition publishes it on its
[Downloadable Forms page](https://www.ssrc.org.nz/downloadable-forms/) for members of the public
to fill in and send to Southland District Council. The text is the coalition's; each signed copy
is that sender's own submission. There are two versions:

- **Non-residents** (this file's `url`) — opens "I write as a concerned member of the public".
- **Residents** — `https://www.ssrc.org.nz/site_files/55033/upload_files/Section128Residents(1)(1).pdf?dl=1`,
  opens "I write as a resident of … who lives in proximity to the Datagrid hyperscale data centre
  and its GXP substation". Otherwise the grounds and requests are the same, numbered rather than
  bulleted.

**The `date` is inferred.** Neither PDF is dated. It asks the council to act "before the statutory
deadline of 5 September 2026". The council's 5 August 2026 report first gave that deadline as
11 September and corrected it to 5 September in a post-meeting note (`official/sdc-review-request-2026-08`).
The form uses the corrected date, so it was written between **5 August and 5 September 2026**;
2026-08-05 is the earliest defensible date, not a known one.

**The deadline it names has passed.** As at retrieval the council's decision on whether to open
the review was due ten days earlier. That outcome is not yet in the corpus.

**The coalition's full submission is linked but not retrieved.** The form points readers to "The
Southland Sustainable Resource Coalition's Section 128 submission" at a Facebook post
(`https://www.facebook.com/permalink.php?story_fbid=pfbid02K3BQssoTWXLCZ4cnWt5j8qZxvaAgUYmYcA2hGFUDct7nZptqmyoQJq7J7AeuJCwul&id=61590982592338`).
Facebook refuses automated requests; it needs opening in a browser. That post is the document
`notes/information-requests.md` has been chasing.

**Addressees:** the form is addressed to Cameron McIntosh and Elizabeth Hovell of Southland

## Summary

The Southland Sustainable Resource Coalition's own statement of why Resource Consent
RMA/2025/53212 should be reviewed under s128 of the RMA. It turns the council's one-line summary
of "concerns primarily relating to the acoustic assessment" into eight specific technical
criticisms of the noise assessment, ties them to the non-notified process, and asks for
independent low-frequency noise and infrasound modelling. The coalition's site frames the
review more widely than the form, adding decommissioning security, e-waste and non-notification.

## Relevant quotes

The requests, as first put:

> "I ask the Council to (1) acknowledge my submission, (2) initiate a Section 128 review, (3)
> undertake an appropriate and complete assessment of all noise effects, and (4) re-identify and
> engage with all affected parties."

Why the affected parties are unknown:

> "Because the original noise assessment was inadequate, it is currently unknown who will be
> affected. This uncertainty is precisely why a Section 128 review is required. The consent was
> granted on a non-notified basis -- there was no formal process to identify and consult all
> affected parties. Without proper low-frequency noise and infrasound modelling, we cannot know the
> full extent of people that fall within the affected zone."

The eight criticisms of the noise assessment:

> "I request the Council initiate a Section 128 review before the statutory deadline of 5
> September 2026, as the noise assessment contains critical deficiencies:
> • Wrong primary noise source modelled (chillers, not free-air cooling fans which run >80% of the
> time);
> • No low-frequency noise (LFN) or infrasound assessment;
> • HV substation badly under-specified (only "2 transformers" modelled, no frequency data);
> • Soil acoustic properties not assessed i.e. no Ground Factor specified under ISO 9613-2;
> • Atmospheric inversions not considered;
> • Baseline monitoring insufficient (7 days, one season, one position, A-weighted only);
> • No assessment of how nearby homes block or transmit noise (no window/glazing data for existing
> dwellings);
> • No independent peer review."

On the rural setting:

> "A rural environment with very low night background levels cannot absorb an unassessed 24/7
> industrial noise source without risk to sleep, health, and quality of life."

On the consent system:

> "The integrity of the RMA process requires that consents are based on complete information and
> that affected persons are properly identified. These deficiencies represent a risk not only to
> nearby residents but to public trust in the consent system."

The requests, as finally put:

> "I ask the Council to: (1) acknowledge my submission; (2) initiate the review under s 128(1)(c)
> of the RMA; (3) require independent peer-reviewed LFN/infrasound modelling and continuous
> monitoring; and (4) re-identify and notify all affected persons."

Non-residents version only, on precedent:

> "Given the unprecedented scale of this development (New Zealand's first hyperscale facility) and
> the precedent it sets for future infrastructure, I believe it is imperative that a thorough and
> transparent process be followed from the outset."

### The coalition's framing on the page that publishes the form

From [ssrc.org.nz/downloadable-forms](https://www.ssrc.org.nz/downloadable-forms/), under "Why a
Section 128?", read 2026-09-15:

> "The Southland District Council has a statutory duty to protect ratepayers from unfunded
> environmental and health liabilities. The missing noise baseline monitoring from July 2026,
> combined with the absence of decommissioning security, e-waste tracking provisions, and the
> procedural concerns around non-notification, collectively justify immediate Section 128 review
> action."

> "The international precedents demonstrate that speculative data centre projects can fail, leaving
> communities with abandoned industrial sites, contamination liabilities, and remediation costs. A
> Section 128 review provides the appropriate mechanism to address financial, health, and
> environmental safeguard gaps before they crystallise into actual harm."

## Claims this supports

- `claim-makarewa-consent-review` — the coalition's grounds and its four requests under s128(1)(c)
