## Summary

The national denominator. Supplies total New Zealand electricity generation and consumption,
which is what any "X per cent of the country's electricity" claim about Makarewa has to be
measured against, and the current renewable share against which the application's 82% figure
is stale.

## Relevant quotes

Renewable share:

> "The share of renewables in electricity generation reached 88.0 per cent in 2023, up by 1 per
> cent on 2022's previous record high since the early 1980s. This increase was due to higher
> levels of electricity generation from hydro, wind, and solar, leading to less reliance on
> coal- and gas-fired generation to meet demand."

Total generation, and the mix:

> "Total electricity generation stayed steady in 2023 at 43,488 GWh. Hydro generation, which
> made up 60.5 per cent of net generation in 2023, increased by 1.1 per cent (296 GWh) to
> 26,309 GWh."

> "Wind generation increased by 13.0 per cent (369 GWh) to 3,206 GWh, with new capacity coming"
> … "Geothermal generation was down by 3.4 per cent (275 GWh) to 7,758 GWh." … "Gas generation
> was down 4.9 per cent (212 GWh) to 4,097 GWh, while coal generation dropped by 17.6 per cent
> (220 GWh) to 1,031 GWh."

National consumption:

> "National electricity consumption was also relatively unchanged, up 0.2 per cent (69 GWh) to
> 39,130 GWh in 2023. For the first time, residential consumption surpassed industrial
> consumption to become the largest sector of electricity consumption in New Zealand."

Industrial consumption, relevant to the Tiwai comparison:

> "Industrial electricity consumption decreased by 5 per cent (673 GWh) in 2023 to 12,903 GWh."
